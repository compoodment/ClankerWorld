Households can gather clay and make reusable water jugs and food storage pots
at home. Adults fill jugs beside rivers and lakes, then carry the water home;
the sea cannot supply fresh water. A pot slows spoilage for the food actually
inside it. Building storage and agent inspection show vessel contents.

The trial recipe uses two clay and one wood per vessel. Each holds eight items;
filling a jug takes six periods of work and a pot halves its contents' spoilage.
These numbers are provisional. Consuming water or food keeps the vessel, and
carrying or trading a filled vessel moves its contents with it.
